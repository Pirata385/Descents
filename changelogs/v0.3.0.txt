DESCENTS — version 0.3.0
Date: 2026-10-13
Layer 3, the Great Fault, explorable from top to bottom; the entrance of
Layer 4; a rebuilt creature system; PS4 controller support; deep-layer
lighting and sound; graphics presets and performance work.

LAYER 3 — THE GREAT FAULT (900–1900 m)
- The fault is now a chasm about 300 m across and a kilometre deep, from the
  Stone Plain to the entrance of Layer 4. Its shape is procedural: it drifts,
  narrows and widens, its walls are warped and banded, and its depth zones
  (ember reach, rust strata, lantern deeps) have their own rock, light, fog,
  particles and ambience.
- The staircase descent (the Threshold Descent) is gone. The way down is a
  natural cave network in the chasm walls: swallets in the plain open into
  passages that traverse, meander, switch back and corkscrew down from hall
  to hall. Two trunks cross, merge and loop; side passages, dead-end dens and
  cross links make it a network rather than a corridor.
- Halls are named chambers (vaults, galleries, grottos); windows break out
  of the walls onto ledges over the void; natural shelves, nest ledges and
  roosts line the walls.
- At the bottom: the Basin, a cavern floor of dark pools and pale fungus, and
  the Fourth Gate, the entrance of Layer 4, the Lantern Sea (1900 m).
- The planner checks every passage for walkability (step height, headroom,
  floor, grade), checks that the network connects the plain to the Basin and
  the Gate, and removes passages that cannot be made walkable.
- Maps: the Great Fault has three map bands (upper, middle, lower) plus a
  Layer 4 band, the chasm outline is drawn, and new landmark types (swallets,
  halls, windows, rookeries, the Basin, the Fourth Gate) are discovered and
  charted. The vertical map reaches 2000 m.
- New artifact sites: dens, windows, nest ledges and the Fourth Gate.

CREATURES — REBUILT FROM SCRATCH
- Genome: 49 continuous genes with a species mean and spread, plus allele
  traits (ear shape, horns, crest, tail tip, pattern, eyes, spike rows,
  cheeks). No two individuals of a species look the same.
- Twelve body plans (quadruped, biped, sprawler, serpent, bird, bat, glider,
  insect, spider, centipede, golem, roller) turn genes into proportions,
  features, natural palettes per layer and abilities.
- Subspecies: every species has two or three regional forms with their own
  gene offsets and allele preferences, chosen by depth, zone and sector; no
  variant is defined by hand. Hybrids occur where ranges meet.
- Inheritance: each gene from either parent or a blend, plus mutation;
  regions keep pools of genomes, so the creatures you meet descend from the
  ones you met before.
- One skinned, flat-shaded mesh per individual (one draw call) with painted
  patterns and glowing markings in the deep.
- Procedural animation: gaits per body plan with two-bone IK foot placement,
  wing flaps, folds and glides, head tracking, breathing, feeding, calls,
  threat displays and courtship.
- Behaviour: needs (hunger, energy, fear, mating, the group) and a
  temperament from the genes drive a utility AI — forage, hunt, flee,
  scavenge, court, nest, raise young, flock, mob predators, defend
  territory, live beside hosts, investigate the explorer, sleep.
- Habitats: ground, cave passages, chasm walls, open air, perches and
  roosts. Food webs are built per layer, with mobbing and symbiosis.
- Layer 3 has only the requested fauna: small lizards and mammals in the
  caves (skinks, lichen geckos, cave shrews, tunnel ferrets) and birds,
  lizards and flying mammals in the chasm (wall-nesting swifts, falcons,
  wall-climbing and gliding lizards, rift bats, flying foxes) that nest and
  roost on its walls.
- Creatures of the surface and Layers 1 and 2 never attack the explorer, and
  a quiet observer is gradually tolerated. In the Great Fault, nesting
  falcons and wall lizards defend their eggs and dive at an explorer who
  comes too close.
- The journal shows each species' niche, regional forms, traits and how it
  raises its young; the bestiary view (?bestiary=1) shows two parents and
  four of their young per species.

PS4 CONTROLLER
- DualShock 4 support (DualSense and other standard-layout controllers
  work the same): analog movement and look with a response curve and turn
  acceleration, L3 run toggle, face buttons for jump, crouch, take and
  observe, triggers and bumpers for the grappling arm, d-pad for the lamp,
  the artifact ability and the maps, SHARE / touchpad for the journal,
  OPTIONS to pause.
- Menus, settings and the journal are fully navigable: a focus highlight,
  ✕ select, ○ back, L1/R1 journal tabs, right stick scrolling, map pan and
  zoom.
- PlayStation glyphs in prompts and hints while the controller is in use;
  switching back to the keyboard, mouse or touch screen switches back.
- Rumble on hurt, hard landings, death, and when the claw fires and bites.
- Settings: stick look speed, dead zone, vibration. On phones and tablets
  the touch buttons hide while a controller is in use.

RENDERING, LIGHTING AND SOUND
- Deep lighting: below the plain the sun no longer reaches; the fault is lit
  by ember veins, lichen, glowing fungus, light shafts from the plain, the
  creatures' glow and the lamp. Layer 4 has its own cold palette and motes.
- Graphics presets Low / Medium / High / Ultra (Custom when edited) set view
  distance, resolution, shadows and shadow size, particles and creature
  count. Phones start on Low.
- Dynamic resolution: automatic quality lowers the render scale first, then
  the view distance, and restores them when there is headroom.
- Audio: Layer 4 music; a tight cave reverb, a vast chasm reverb and an
  echo off the far wall, mixed by how open the chasm is around you; the
  updraft howling through the fault, scree falls, ticking embers, the chimes
  and lapping water of the Lantern Sea; new creature calls (squeak, chitter,
  scream).

PERFORMANCE AND MEMORY
- The shadow map is redrawn only where the sun reaches (and every other
  frame at 1024 px).
- Distant creatures think, move and animate at a lower rate; strays turn
  back instead of being recycled; new regions are surveyed and new bodies
  built within a per-frame time budget.
- Fault generation: cached chasm wall queries, a cached warp grid and a
  spatial index for clearance checks; the runtime wall cache is capped.

FIXES
- The explorer no longer dies below 1150 m (the old bottom of the world).
- A tap on a phone is no longer swallowed by the input device switch.

SAVES
- Save format version 2. Saves made by 0.2.0 or earlier start their seed
  anew, because the world below the Stone Plain and its creatures changed.
