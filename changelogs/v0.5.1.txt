DESCENTS — version 0.5.1
Date: 2026-10-29
A new sound engine written from scratch, and a heavy optimisation pass for
slow devices: memory no longer grows as you explore, the lowest settings
draw a fraction of what they did, and each frame does less work.

SOUND — A NEW ENGINE
- The old engine is gone. It built a small graph of oscillators, filters and
  3D panner nodes for every sound, ran two convolution reverbs all the time
  and leaned on a compressor; on slow devices that load could choke the
  audio thread, and the sound kept failing.
- The new engine bakes every sound into samples when the game starts, in a
  worker (about 120 sounds and every species' call, under a second of
  work), and plays them back from a pool of at most 24 voices. Nothing is
  synthesised while you play: no convolution, no 3D panner nodes. Where a
  sound comes from becomes its volume and stereo balance, computed from the
  camera; distant sounds pass through one muffling filter; the Great Fault's
  echo is a single delay line. Ambience beds (wind, gusts, lakes, rivers,
  falls, cave air, the deep hum, the chasm's howl, rain in the goblet
  cavern, night insects, the city) fade in and out with the surroundings and
  are stopped when silent. Music plays baked instrument notes (pads, glass,
  choir, bells, plucked strings, chimes) in each layer's scale, with the
  warden's drums during the fight.
- Sound starts with the first click, tap or key press, and any later one
  wakes it if the browser put it to sleep (also when the page comes back
  into view). iPhones and iPads are unlocked with a sound started inside
  the gesture, and sound plays with their ring/silent switch on (older
  versions keep a silent media element playing for that). A context whose
  clock stops is replaced.
- Settings -> Audio -> Sound check plays a test sound and shows the state of
  sound (on, waiting for a tap, paused).

PERFORMANCE
- Memory no longer grows as you explore. Before, up to 360 terrain chunks
  were kept with a copy of their meshes in memory as well as on the GPU:
  measured on the lowest settings, memory climbed from about 100 MB to over
  450 MB after visiting seven places (and several GB in total), which made
  the game slower the longer it ran. Now a chunk's copy is dropped once it is
  on the GPU, only a few hidden chunks are kept and far ones are released at
  once: over the same exploration memory stays between 40 and 130 MB.
- Far fewer triangles on the lowest settings: chunks split by the distance
  of their centre (nearly everything near the camera used to go to full
  detail), chunks beyond the nearest ones are meshed with cells twice as
  large (a quarter of the triangles), vegetation is thinner. On the Low
  preset a typical view went from 240,000-525,000 triangles to about
  90,000-130,000.
- The Great Fault was the heaviest place: a chunk there holds a kilometre of
  wall, and all of it was drawn even when only a few metres were in view
  (200,000-480,000 triangles looking up or down from its caves and windows
  on the Low preset). Tall chunks are now cut into slabs 64 m high and
  slabs out of view or beyond the fog are skipped: the same views draw
  35,000-130,000 triangles, with the same picture (compared pixel by pixel;
  the open sky far above the Fault now fades into the haze like its far
  walls, which is what made the two pictures match).
- Creatures: 20 on the Low preset, with simpler skins (about 2,000 triangles
  instead of 3,400).
- The terrain shader skips its per-pixel rock grain, strata wobble and hex
  outlines on the lowest settings.
- Less work per frame: terrain chunks no longer have their matrices
  recomputed every frame; the creature under the crosshair is found a few
  times a second (every frame only while observing); the compass is rebuilt
  20 times a second; the creatures' spatial grid uses numbers instead of
  strings; the background population model runs a few regions per frame
  instead of all of them every five seconds, and long-unvisited regions are
  forgotten.
- Small devices (4 cores or fewer, 4 GB of memory or less, or the Low
  preset) build chunks with two workers, leaving the cores to the game.
- Presets are lighter: Low = render distance 5, terrain detail 35%,
  resolution 0.6, no shadows or antialiasing, 20 creatures; Medium has no
  shadows. Saved presets (and the old Low values) move to the new ones.
  Terrain detail now goes down to 25% and creatures to 8.

TESTS
- npm run test:perf: a long exploration on the Low preset reporting frame
  cost per system, memory, GPU geometries, chunks and triangles.
- npm run test:audio (rewritten for the new engine): sound starts with the
  first click, the sounds are baked (calls included), ambience and
  footsteps are heard, calls and effects play, a swarm stays within the
  voice budget, pausing and resuming, a suspended context wakes.
- Unit test: coarse far chunks cut the triangles to about a quarter and the
  vegetation thins.
